The intuition behind generating text from large language models is very simple. Given some instruction (prompt), we want to continue with the most probable token. Models such as this usually return logits, which are turned into probabilities using functions like softmax. Now the question remains, how do we choose which of the possible tokens to predict: 
- Greedy decoding (beam search): always take the most probable one. This works well in tasks such as machine translation.
- Random sampling: just randomly choose a token from the distribution. In practice this doesn't work well and this therefore extended by temperature, top-k, and top-p sampling. 
- Temperature sampling: we reshape the probability distribution to in-temperature sampling increase the probability of the high probability tokens and decrease the probability of the low probability tokens. The result is that we are less likely to generate very low-probability tokens, and more likely to generate tokens that are higher probability. We implement this intuition by simply dividing the logit by a temperature parameter $t$ before passing it through the softmax.
- Top-k sapling: just a more sophisticated version of random sampling. Instead of sampling from the complete vocabulary, we truncate the vocabulary to the k-most likely words, normalize the probabilities again and choose according to these probabilities. 
- Top-p sampling: we keep not the top k words, but the top p percent of the probability mass. The goal is the same - to truncate the distribution to remove the very unlikely words. But in this way, the distribution of the probability mass does not matter.
